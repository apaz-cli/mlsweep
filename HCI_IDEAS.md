# Reading and steering agent research

An agent produces more than a person can read, and the person's attention is the scarce
resource. The goal is to make looking at the work cheap, and to let what you notice while
looking turn straight into direction.

## Catching up

- Since you last looked: The Notes tab tracks what you have read and shows only what
  changed since, grouped by note. Git already has the data.
- Levels of detail: One line, then `STATUS.md`, then the notes, then the runs. Each level
  links down to the next, so you stop reading once you trust it.
- Surprises first: Ask agents to report what contradicted their expectations. Progress is
  mostly predictable, and the surprises are where your judgment changes something.

## Checking a claim

- Numbers that open their evidence: Click a number beside an experiment ID to see the
  curve it came from. No checking is automated, it only makes your own checking a click away.
- Fresh reader test: Give a new agent only the notes and ask it what was found and why.
  Where it goes wrong, the note depended on context its author had. This also catches walls of
  text and private shorthand.
- Predict, then look: Write down what you expect before opening a result. Over time this
  shows how well you and the agent each predict, and when to look closer.

## Talking to the agent

- Comments anchored to the work: Select a line, plot or run and leave a comment, as in code
  review. The agent gets the comment with what it points at, so "this looks wrong" is enough.
- Dashboard selection as context: Whatever you are looking at is what "this" means when you
  talk to the agent.
- A question queue: Agents post questions they can't settle to one list, with what they did
  in the meantime. You answer in batches, and nothing has to stop and wait.
- Steering without stopping: A message can wait for the agent's next natural pause, such as
  the end of a sweep, so it changes direction without losing running work.

## Where to start

Anchored comments and "since you last looked". Both build on files and git as they are, and
they cover the two things you do most, reading the notes and talking to the agent.
